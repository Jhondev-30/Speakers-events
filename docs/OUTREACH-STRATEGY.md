## Kill Criteria (When to stop)

**Stop investing in a site if:**
- 6 months of profiles + 10+ submissions = 0 responses
- Fee <$500 AND requires custom application AND no clear contact path
- Site has been down or broken for >2 months
- Geo-blocked AND we can't get a USA-based speaker to verify

**Stop investing in a speaker if:**
- 12 months of outreach = <3 paid events
- Speaker declines or is unresponsive
- Speaker transitions out of speaking business

## Metrics (Monthly Tracking)

- **Submissions sent**: target 30/speaker/year
- **Response rate**: target >15% (industry avg 10%)
- **Booking rate**: target 5-10% of responses
- **Pipeline value**: $ (estimated) of all active opportunities
- **Time to first response**: <2 weeks ideal
- **Fee average**: trending up over time

## Strategic Notes (Strategic Thinker Lens)

**Where the real opportunity is:**
1. **Angie first**: She's the first client. Her success = SpeakUp's case study. Get her 3-5 paid events in 90 days.
2. **Meade's bureaus**: Once in AAS/Premiere, bookings come inbound. Worth the upfront effort.
3. **PodMatch multiplier**: Every speaker can do 5-10 podcasts/year with minimal effort. Easy wins.

**Where to NOT waste time:**
- 50+ events a year (CFP events) take 4-6 weeks response. Don't over-apply.
- Communities like meetup.com require local presence. Angie/Jeff in Orlando can do this; remote speakers can't.
- Local faith events under 100 people are pure exposure. Only do 1-2 for pipeline.

**Trade-offs to accept:**
- Volume vs selectivity: Better to do 5 high-quality submissions than 50 sloppy ones.
- $$ vs exposure: For tier-2 speakers, exposure + travel may be the realistic outcome (not pure $). Plan accordingly.
- Time vs $$: Some sites (AAS, Premiere) take 6+ months to convert. Worth it if speaker has 2-year horizon.

**Kill criteria reminders:**
- If a speaker gets <2 paid events in 12 months of active pipeline → re-evaluate their positioning.
- If a site has <5% response rate across 10+ submissions → stop.
- If the same conference rejected the same speaker 3 years in a row → unlikely to change.
